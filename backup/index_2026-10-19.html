‘Other Desert Cities’ review: Julia Louis-Dreyfus and Allison Janney’s play has laughs, but no bite

“Other Desert Cities,” like so many plays, is about a family with issues, to say the very least. A free-spirited Manhattan writer named Brooke jets to sunny Palm Springs to visit her well-to-do mom, dad, TV producer brother and leftist aunt for Christmas. She’s very New York, they’re very California circa 2004. Her buttoned-up Republican parents were close friends of Ronald and Nancy Reagan from their days working alongside them in Hollywood — mom as a screenwriter with her sister, and dad as an actor like Ronnie. Now, their downcast daughter has brought along a surprise lump of coal as a present: galleys of her new memoir, which airs their dirtiest laundry. Those toxic pages have the potential to destroy them. But the likeable enough Broadway revival of Jon Robin Baitz’s sharp-tongued drama, which opened Sunday night at the Hudson Theatre, takes these people’s competing personalities way too far. Quite by accident, everybody in this midcentury modern home — not just Brooke — becomes the black sheep. Director John Benjamin Hickey’s discordant production starring Julia Louis-Dreyfus, Allison Janney, Ed Harris, Joe Keery and Lily Rabe fails to click when it’s anything more than one person tossing off mean zingers. Each actor has their moments, most are fine and one is excellent. Not for a second, however, does anybody believe that the Wyeths are a real family or something other than an impressive assemblage of celebrities.
