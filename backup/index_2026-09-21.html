New generation breathes life back into shrinking Little Italy in time for Feast of San Gennaro’s 100th anniversary: ‘Change is inevitable’

Once a sprawling 30-block enclave of Italian immigrants, the neighborhood has shrunk to five blocks along Mulberry Street between Canal and Broome. But now, as Little Italy celebrates the 100th anniversary of its famed Feast of San Gennaro, a new generation of personalities and businesses is moving in — bringing fresh energy to a neighborhood that many feared was fading away. Instead, the old guard is holding the door open, hoping newcomers breathe new life into the beloved enclave — and keep Little Italy alive for another generation. “The neighborhood is currently reinventing itself, as it’s done constantly,” Ernest Lepore, 61, whose family has run Grand Street’s Ferrara Bakery and Cafe since 1892, told The Post. “From a businessman’s point of view, the crowds bring a coolness to the neighborhood,” he mused. “I’m thrilled when I see the young people. We welcome them all; it’s how the neighborhood reinvents itself. It’s an evolution, and I’ve been waiting for the next wave of fun.” Part of that new wave is Massimo Laveglia, whose acclaimed L’Industrie Pizza landed next door to Ferrara Bakery in March. Born in Pistoia, Tuscany, Laveglia brought his viral pizza joint to Grand Street, opening its third location after Williamsburg and the West Village. Since then, it’s been pizza pandemonium, with hungry fans lining up daily and spilling around the block. “We found an opportunity and took a chance,” says Laveglia of his shop, which slings out $47 pies topped with imported ingredients like prosciutto di Parma and burrata. Regularly voted one of the best pizza shops in the USA, it was awarded the tasty moniker of Pizzeria of the Year from PizzaToday.com. “It’s not that we’re trying to elevate Little Italy, it’s been here forever, but they all know they needed to change, and maybe it’s the beginning of something good,” he said, alluding to a previous lack of foot traffic and pandemic-related slowdown. On nearby Mott Street, gourmet pizzeria Ceres has been wooing foodies and earning acclaim since opening in January 2025, with meticulously crafted pies priced at upwards of $60. And just blocks away, another newcomer has arrived. Acclaimed West Village aperitivo bar Dante opened an Elizabeth Street outpost earlier this month, bringing Italian-inspired cocktails and fare to a stretch that was once at the heart of old Little Italy. Dante is part of a larger transformation underway on the street. Michelin-starred Italian restaurant Torrisi has been drawing diners to Elizabeth Street since December 2022, despite its reputation for being notoriously difficult to book.
