Rare anti-government protests erupt in Iran over workers' deaths

Iranian protesters in the southern Iranian town of Abdanan chant 'death to the dictators!" during a protest after 13 oil refinery workers were killed in a road accident over the weekend. Credit: The Associated Press. Hundreds of Iranians took to the streets chanting "Death to the dictator!" and other anti-government messages in the wake of a deadly bus crash over the weekend that killed 13 employees of an oil refinery company, reports said. The rare display of anti-government anger unfolded Tuesday in Abdanan, a city in western Iran near the country's border with Iraq. Demonstrators called for the resignation of company officials blamed for the accident. "Those who seek to exploit this incident to disrupt public order and inflame people’s emotions will be dealt with decisively," local prosecutor Amrollah Meysaei was quoted by Iran's Mizan news agency as saying, according to Radio Free Europe. The outlet, citing Iran's Fars news agency, also reported that an estimated 1,000 people took part in the protests, making them the largest demonstrations since mass unrest swept across Iran at the beginning of this year. Some of the protesters reportedly tried to damage public property.
