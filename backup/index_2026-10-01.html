Padres make insane MLB history in sweep over Cubs in wild-card

The Padres swept the Cubs in the wild-card series and made history while doing so. They defeated Chicago 4-1 on Wednesday night to complete the sweep and advance. In doing so, San Diego became the first team in MLB history to hold the highest-scoring team in the majors during the regular season to just one run (or none) in a multi-game postseason series, according to OptaSTATS. The Cubs lineup mustered just a single run throughout the series, with likely NL MVP Pete Crow-Armstrong and Alex Bregman going a combined 0-for-15. The previous low for runs scored by the regular-season scoring leader in a multi-game postseason series was three (the Orioles held the White Sox to three runs in the 1983 ALCS). The Padres pitching staff was dominant against Chicago, tossing 18 innings of one-run baseball across two games with a 0.50 ERA, 0.83 WHIP, eight hits allowed and 19 strikeouts. The red hot Padres squad spent Wednesday night celebrating in the clubhouse with new owners, Kwanza Jones and Jose E. Feliciano. But it’s back to business for the Padres as they have a date with the Brewers in Milwaukee. The NL Division Series between the Padres and Brewers kicks off Saturday at American Family Field. While San Diego has continued its hot streak into October, the Brewers ended with season going 15-3 and finished the year with the best record in the league.

Howell) is a teen girl who grew up in seclusion, raised by her strict mother, Margaret (Samantha Sloyan). When she attends high school for the first time, the sheltered girl struggles to navigate social cues. Bullying ensues, and her telekinetic abilities start acting up. The whole thing culminates in a prom that doesn’t go well – to say the least. None of that is a spoiler, since “Carrie” has been a best seller since 1974, and the movie starring Sissy Spacek came out in 1976. (It also got a swiftly forgotten 2013 remake starring  Chloë Grace Moretz and Julianne Moore). The bloody prom scene in “Carrie” is as iconic in pop culture history as the “Psycho” shower scene. Wisely, the show acknowledges it right out of the gate and doesn’t pretend the audience is ignorant about what the big climax is. The move could have made the show predictable, but instead, it uses our knowledge to make the journey to that destination surprising and full of mounting tension. “Carrie’s” scaffolding is familiar, but the structure and decor on top are shiny and new as the series gets a contemporary setting with social media, online message boards, and a moody Lana Del Rey soundtrack.
