Is watching horror movies a sin? A Christian makes the case for fear

Allie Beth Stuckey's warning about frightening entertainment raises a larger question: Can horror serve a redemptive purpose? This simple question has sparked a massive debate in Christian circles. It all started when "Relatable" host Allie Beth Stuckey raised concerns about believers consuming entertainment designed to terrify them. Movies like 'Nefarious' and 'The Conjuring' set up battles between good and evil, showcasing what happens when the demonic realm bursts into our lives. Whether you agree with Stuckey’s argument that we’re not meant to watch content that stokes fear in our hearts, the conversation — especially as Halloween looms — is worth having. At first glance, the simplest response to the horror movie question is a whopping: It depends. Christians often have different convictions, and even seemingly simple questions should be met with prayer and action based on the convictions that come from seeking God’s will. That said, I think it’s essential to go a bit deeper. Christians are called to guard our hearts and minds, taking great care with what we consume. Matthew 6:22-23 offers a stark warning many of us overlook when we watch movies, listen to music, or consume content that bends moral boundaries: "The eye is the lamp of the body. If your eyes are healthy, your whole body will be full of light. But if your eyes are unhealthy, your whole body will be full of darkness. If then the light within you is darkness, how great is that darkness!" The Bible is clear that we’re meant to keep our hearts and minds pure and focused on the Lord. But what does that mean for entertainment that deliberately frightens us or confronts us with evil? Does depicting something disturbing necessarily make a movie spiritually harmful? I depart from Stuckey on the fear front. I have no problem watching content that stokes fear in my heart, so long as that fear serves a redemptive purpose. Sometimes fear is a storytelling tool that helps us understand the seriousness of evil, drawing us closer to our need for a Savior. But I also agree with much of what Stuckey said about the dangers of some horror films. Much of the horror content on the market today is simply designed to disturb and perturb the mind, offering little more than jitters and insanely unpalatable imagery. The intent is simply to terrify, with no real purpose beyond the shock itself. I’m not interested in that sort of content, and I don’t think it is honorable or helpful to the Christian mind.
