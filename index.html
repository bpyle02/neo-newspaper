Dolly Parton's path from Nashville newcomer to Billboard's No. 1 country music artist of all time

Steve Doocy joins 'Fox and Friends' to reflect on the life, career and charitable work of music icon Dolly Parton following her death at age 80. They discuss her music catalog, her Imagination Library literacy program and her Dollywood theme park in Tennessee. During that time, the legendary country music star cemented herself as an icon not only in the music industry, but also as a movie star and a philanthropist. From getting her start on "The Porter Wagoner Show" in 1964 to multiple Academy Award nominations, Parton forged a path for herself in the industry and paved the way for female country singers who followed her. Parton moved to Nashville, Tennessee, in May 1964, the day after graduating from high school, to pursue a career in country music. Three years later, she released her debut album, "Hello, I'm Dolly," in February 1967, which caught the attention of singer Porter Wagoner. He then invited Parton to join him on his TV show, "The Porter Wagoner Show," a few months later, in September 1967. Parton's time on the show provided her with national attention and established her as a recording artist. Her duets with Wagoner, starting with "The Last Thing on My Mind," became major country hits. Their partnership also earned Parton her first Grammy nomination in 1970.
