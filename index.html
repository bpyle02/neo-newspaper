Gavin Newsom Commutes 548 Death Sentences to Life in Prison

California Gov. Gavin Newsom announced Thursday that he will commute every death sentence in the state to life in prison without the possibility of parole. The clemency covers 548 people, including 529 men and 19 women, and represents the largest mass commutation of death sentences in U.S. history. Newsom published an open letter explaining his decision. “I take this action because I firmly believe that state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California’s values, undermines the foundations of our legal system, and diminishes each of us,” he wrote. The governor further argued that “we cannot execute people in our imperfect legal system and remain true to our core values, first among them justice and human decency” and that he “could not remain true to my values and oversee the killing of people with death warrants in California. He added, “We can now let these offenders die in obscurity while we forever remember and honor their victims.” The governor also responded to suggestions that he only preserve the death penalty for the “worst of the worst” and explained why he could not go this route. Some have said I should not grant clemency to people who committed the "worst of the worst" crimes. I carefully considered different categories of capital crimes and offender characteristics. I have learned, however, from speaking with the victims harmed by these crimes, that each experiences their loss as the worst of the worst. I have concluded that attempting to distinguish among crimes and offenders for clemency would inadvertently create two classes of murder victims—those whose perpetrators deserve the death penalty and then the rest.
