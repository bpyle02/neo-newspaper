Newsom’s death-row commutations spark DA revolt as prosecutors warn of legal fight

,Former DOJ prosecutor Jim Trusty reacts to Gov. Gavin Newsom's plan to commute hundreds of death sentences, criticism from victims' families and questions about the decision's timing. He also weighs in on the federal investigations into Rep. Ilhan Omar's fraud allegeations. At least a dozen California district attorneys are publicly condemning Gov. Gavin Newsom’s plan to commute all 548 death sentences in the state, arguing the sweeping move overrides juries, disregards victims’ families and defies the will of state voters. Newsom announced the plan Thursday, saying state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California’s values. The move would convert the sentences to life without parole, though 382 cases still require approval from at least four California Supreme Court justices, according to the Associated Press. In response, prosecuting attorneys across California have raised concerns about victims’ rights, the governor’s constitutional authority and the role of the California Supreme Court in reviewing hundreds of the commutations. "Justice requires case-by-case consideration, not one-size-fits-all decisions," Sacramento County District Attorney Thien Ho said in response to Newsom's action. A table fitted with restraints holds down death row prisoners while they are given a lethal injection at Huntsville Unit (Walls Unit) in Huntsville, Texas. (Greg Smith/CORBIS/Corbis via Getty Images) Orange County District Attorney Todd Spitzer described the move as "mercy for the wolves" that inflicts "the ultimate act of cruelty on the sheep." Spitzer noted that regulations implemented by California’s Board of Parole Hearings on Oct. 1 allow thousands of prisoners serving life without parole to become eligible for parole, arguing that Newsom's latest death row commutations could put these inmates "one step closer" to release. Newsom’s office says, however, that the affected prisoners will remain in prison and are not eligible for release. "The Governor is attempting to appear courageous with this decision. He is not courageous. His commutations are solely based upon power and political aspirations," added San Bernardino County District Attorney Jason Anderson, who accused Newsom of caring more about politics than people. "Courage would require speaking with crime victims and the surviving family members of the people murdered by these death row inmates."
