Trump Is Right: Veering From MAGA Costs Republicans

President Donald Trump said that people who veer from MAGA “don’t do very well,” and he’s right — Republicans lost both chambers of Congress in a massive blue sweep after Trump started a new war that caused gas prices to skyrocket while Americans at home struggled with affordability. The president was responding to Elon Musk, who said on Wednesday that he doesn’t consider himself “MAGA.” “I’m not MAGA … Well, at least I’m not like … I’m not sure what MAGA means,” Musk said “If that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don’t have that.” Trump responded to the comments — which were made by Musk while he was traveling with the president — saying “He’s a friend of mine … there are a lot of definitions out there. MAGA, to me means something very specific, and when people veer from it, they don’t do very well.” As The Federalist CEO and co-founder Sean Davis said, “Trump is right. The election results prove it. When Republicans veer from MAGA, for example by starting new Middle East wars that jack up gas prices, Republicans get punished by voters.” Trump used his farewell address in 2021 to tout the peace deals his administration negotiated in the Middle East. “I am especially proud to be the first president in decades who has started no new wars,” the president said. Four years later, Trump was still making the case. In his 2024 victory speech, he recalled the warnings that he would start a new war during his first administration: “I’m not going to start a war,” he said. “I’m going to stop wars.” Trump allies who shared his opposition to new foreign wars feared in 2025 that the strikes against Iran that summer would turn into longer commitments in the region. But after months went on with no escalation, Trump was applauded for withstanding the pressure from the military-industrial complex and its adherents on both sides of the aisle. Then, in early 2026, pro-war factions lobbied Trump hard to reignite the conflict in Iran, with interventionists like the late Sen. Lindsey Graham and former George W. Bush speechwriter Marc Thiessen personally making the case to the president, as the Wall Street Journal reported. In the end, the neocons got the war they wanted, and American voters felt that the war dragged the Trump administration and its energy away from the MAGA priorities and promises they voted for in 2024.
